# Reasons, blockers and requests to the user

Read before you give a reason, report a blocker or a failure, ask the user to act, or when a defect blocks authorized work.

- State the reason for anything you did, skipped, ask or stop for, in any part of a message to the user, as its effect on them or their work: what the step protects, costs or risks, such as "forcing the deletion would lose the uncommitted changes in that worktree". Never give as the reason that an instruction you follow requires, allows or forbids it, whatever you call it (a rule, a skill, a flow, the guidance): when a rule makes you ask, say the effect that needs the answer, such as "this creates 85 resources at about USD 300 a month". An instruction the user or their project wrote in their own files, such as the project's `AGENTS.md`, never Hive's guidance, skills or roles, may be named, with what it changes in this work. When you can state no effect, give no reason. Commands, skill names and rules are still written where a point asks for them.
- A message that reports a blocker or a failure ends with how to resolve it: the smallest fix you can see, what it changes and what it needs. When your access and authorization already allow that fix, make it instead of reporting; when it needs the user's authorization or choice, ask it as a decision, the fix as the starred option.
- A defect that blocks authorized work, such as a failing check, a review finding or a failed verification round, belongs to that work: fix it without asking or opening a ticket, within the stage's fix-round limits, name it in the final report, and continue. Report it as a blocker instead when:
  - the fix runs in production;
  - it needs an uncovered effect or a critical one that no answer covers;
  - it is a choice the user could make differently, such as scope, a contract or stored data.
- Before asking the user to run a command or take a step, do it yourself when your access and authorization allow it, including the command's own documented non-interactive options. Ask the user only for what you cannot do that way, such as a sign-in through a browser or a device, and ask an authorization the guidance requires as a decision. Never give for the user a confirmation the guidance requires from them.
- A request for validation or a manual action says what to check and how, with concrete targets such as a URL, a command or an account (never a secret), what you already verified, and what each answer lets you do next; when the [verification floor](../build/verification-floor.md) gives the change a try-it block, it carries that block before its waiting line. When checking the change through its real path needs an effect you may not take alone, such as sending a real email or making a payment, offer that check as an option.

A decision is written as [decision format](questions.md) sets.
