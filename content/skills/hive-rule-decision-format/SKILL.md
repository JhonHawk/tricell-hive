# Decision format

Read before you write a decision, a finding, a blocker or a validation request for the user, or any list of points shown to them.

- Ask only for a choice that is the user's and changes what happens next: find the facts yourself, state minor calls as assumptions (a minor call is a choice where every option leaves the scope, contracts, stored data, cost and reversibility unchanged), and reuse decisions already given. When several decisions are open, ask in rounds: each round holds every decision whose prerequisites are settled, until none remains open. Another open decision is a prerequisite when any of its answers would change, remove or make meaningless this question or its options, as it is for a question about carrying out one of its options, typically the recommended one: ask this question in a later round, worded for the answer given, unless its options can be written to hold under every answer, such as "with the review you choose in D2".
- A message that asks for a decision or validation, delivers a result or a blocker, or closes work opens with a bold context line, stage · ticket · progress, such as **Plan · COM-833 · ready**, and ends with one line that says what you are waiting for; an open question about what the user wants, which [the question channel](../hive-rule-question-channel/SKILL.md) sets, needs neither.
- Give each decision an ID, `D1`, `D2`…, on its own title line that holds the question itself, such as `D2 — Do you accept the mobile search width?`, never a bare topic. Under it write a `Situation:` line: what happened or what you found, what you verified and what to check, with its target, in the user's words; name no ID, file, branch or internal name the same message does not explain. Then give each option a line of its own: a letter, a dash and its name, the recommended option first with `(★)` after the dash, such as `A - (★) Accept`, and under it an `If you choose it:` line saying what the user gets and what it costs them in time, money, risk or what stops working. Close the decision with an `I recommend <letter>:` line whose reason is a consequence for the user, as [reasons and blockers](../hive-rule-reasons-and-blockers/SKILL.md) sets; for a choice that changes scope, a contract, stored data or cost, or is hard to reverse, add what remains uncertain. Write the three labels in the session language, and say in the round's waiting line how many decisions you know are still to come. State as fact only what you verified in this session; mark the rest as an assumption. Findings are `H1`, `H2`…, plan tasks `T<n>` and acceptance criteria `AC<n>`. Use these IDs and the star in every language; the numbers run on through the session and are never reused. Name an option by its decision and letter, such as `D2 B`, and mention each ID with a short gloss, such as "D2 (autonomous CI)".
- In a list, report, close or list of pending items shown to the user, each point carries one task; a point with an ID opens with it in bold, followed by a dash and what it is in plain words, such as `- **D2** — Move the plan summary to the top`, so the reader finds it by its ID and understands it without knowing the code, and a point without an ID opens with that plain-words description. A decision's title line and its options keep the format above, and an ID cited inside a sentence keeps its gloss in either order.
