---
title: Close
description: Wrap up a finished, paused or abandoned piece of work, report what happened and clean up its resources and the merged branches left in the repositories it touched.
---

Close is the last step of a piece of work. It moves the ticket, tells you where things stand with the one final report of the work and, in most closes, ends with a footer of replies. At the merge it cleans up before its report: the merged branch and its clean working copy, your earlier merged branches, the task's processes and temporary files, and the evidence the ticket or the pull request already holds. Only what needs your answer waits, such as a working copy holding files that exist nowhere else, a dump or export, or a resource outside your machine. The plan file stays where it is as local support material; the pull request body is the work's public record, and nothing is archived.

## When to use

Close starts when work reaches its completion point: the merge into the base branch, or the open pull request when the delivery is `ready` (it stops before the merge for you) or a person asked to review it, or who reviewed it, has not approved its latest commit: the agent does not wait for that approval, it stops and tells you the merge is pending and on whom. When you already let it merge with `--admin` earlier in the session, that permission lasts the session for the work's pull requests whose required checks passed, or whose base requires none: it merges past an approval given to an earlier commit and names the reviewer, but still stops for a reviewer you requested, for requested changes and for a merge into your production branch. With the pull request left open, the ticket stays in progress, the viewer shows the work as in review, and a later session that finds the pull request merged finishes only what waited for the merge: the viewer close, the ticket and the branch cleanup. It also runs when a one-off operation that leaves nothing in the repository ends, such as a database dump, when you abandon or pause work, when you end the session, or when you ask for a cleanup. Work kept on hold, with nothing committed, is fully closed later, when it is delivered; until then it is paused: the processes and containers it started are stopped and the report lists what is pending. Work paused outside `hold`, such as a plan paused before its build, closes the same way, with its plan file left unversioned.

Checking, cleaning up, reorganizing or migrating documentation and support material that earlier work left, in a repository, a workspace or a monorepo, is not part of Close: the [project-layout](https://github.com/JhonHawk/tricell-hive/blob/master/content/skills/project-layout/SKILL.md) skill does it from a plan you confirm. Close handles only the material of the work it closes, and asks nothing about it: it deletes the evidence that already reached the ticket or the merged pull request, keeps the rest unversioned in your main checkout, and names in the report any conclusion worth moving into the documentation, with the project-layout command. The work's plan file stays in its local work folder, marked completed. Close never commits or archives that material. When the repository still holds old versioned material, such as a `sessions/`, `evidence/` or `archive/` folder, Close names how many items and how much space in one line, with the project-layout command, and does not touch it.

Verification evidence is not kept in Git. While the work is open it stays in the work's unversioned folder, and once the work is merged its pull request, which lists how each acceptance criterion was shown and holds the uploaded before and after captures, is the record. Close deletes a local capture or video when the ticket's attachment list or the merged pull request's body shows its uploaded copy; any other capture stays, and the report names it. What later work reuses, and so is worth moving into the documentation, is: a measurement a later change will be compared against, a rehearsal or procedure that was run, or a review whose findings open other work. Each goes in with its figures, method, date and provenance.

Example requests:

```text wrap
Close this work item and clean up only the temporary resources it created.
Pause this work. Leave the plan on disk and tell me what is still open.
Clean up the session.
```

For E2E work, the coordinator protects branches with active or uncertain ownership during cleanup and reports retained work. A verified local integration is reported separately from publication; it does not by itself authorize deleting a dirty worktree or an unmerged branch. See [E2E coordination](https://github.com/JhonHawk/tricell-hive/blob/master/content/skills/flow-build/references/e2e-coordination.md).

## What it asks

At the completion point it closes and reads back the viewer record when the work has captures there (or leaves it in review, paused or awaiting validation with one update), moves the ticket when integrated, cleans up, and reports, then stops at a footer, not a card, that lists replies from the work's context. An item that needs your answer waits with its own question for the footer's close reply, another work item or the end of the session; when you end the session (for example, *es todo*), the agent cleans up again and its last message lists what still waits. If the session just stops, the next close or cleanup request picks it up. Paused work, work on hold and work waiting at an open pull request only list their support material. The footer recommends seeing the findings when one is P1 or higher, otherwise continuing a plan in progress, otherwise closing while something is left to run. Its other replies come from the context: resolve named findings in the work's branch, see the findings, explain a finding or result, turn a repetition that two sessions have now seen into a script, lint, test or hook of the project, take the next ticket, or continue with what remains. The findings table shows each defect the work found but did not fix with the decision the agent took: fixed in the review's round, listed, or part of one ticket proposal. The findings round asks only what that sorting leaves to you: the ticket proposal, and each finding at a security boundary, on stored data or on a contract, with fix now, ticket or ignore. A listed finding is fixed only when you name it. Fixing now is recommended for a small fix in the area the work touched; a ticket, for work that will be done later or by someone else, after checking that none exists and linked to the ticket it came from, and written as [Create issue](/flows/create-issue/) sets. A ticket is never opened without your answer. Under [unattended delegation](https://github.com/JhonHawk/tricell-hive/blob/master/content/skills/unattended-delegation/SKILL.md) the whole close runs without deferring any step and ends on the report, without the footer; there the agent takes the option it would recommend for each finding's ticket, within that mode's limits on what it may delete, and the report presents each one with how to revert it. When nothing waits for your answer, the footer leaves out closing, so you are not asked to close twice; with no finding of P1 or higher and no plan in progress, the footer is headed Closed and recommends nothing. Like every block of lettered replies, the footer carries an ID, such as D9, so you can answer or cite it by that ID and a letter. Close runs once: after your reply, the agent acts on it and does not repeat the report.

## What it produces

- The work's business rules written to its ticket at the work's completion point, as a section of the description and a comment, without asking: the rules you decided or the agent assumed, and the ones a decision cancelled, each with its reason. Nothing is written when the work has no ticket, was abandoned, or when the section would not change; the final report then lists the rules. A rule card or an approved frame that a later decision left out of date is named in that report as left open, and running [Create design](/flows/create-design/) again for the work corrects it. The [business rules reference](https://github.com/JhonHawk/tricell-hive/blob/master/content/skills/hive-core/business/business-ticket.md) holds the write.
- The ticket documented with the work's evidence, such as its captures, test output and pull request, before anything is cleaned up, so those files reach the ticket while they still exist. Secrets and account identifiers are left out. How each tracker moves, comments and attaches is in the [trackers rule](https://github.com/JhonHawk/tricell-hive/blob/master/content/skills/hive-core/tracker/trackers.md), which reads a ticket's allowed transitions before every move and keeps the project's state names in persistent memory. When `acli` cannot make a Jira write, such as an attachment, the agent uses the Atlassian MCP when your host has it, then Jira's REST API with a token you save once in `~/.config/hive/secrets/jira-api-token`; it asks you to create that file when it is missing and never writes it.
- The time each ticket took, in a tracker that records time, such as Jira: one worklog added at the delivery point and updated here, holding only the time, with no comment. It counts the session's active time from the first message on the ticket, leaving out only the time spent waiting for you, measured by `hive time` from the host's transcript or from the agent's own marks; the agent's work counts twice, as an estimate of a developer's time, and remote CI waits once. The real time and the factor stay in persistent memory for a later recalibration. The [time reference](https://github.com/JhonHawk/tricell-hive/blob/master/content/skills/hive-core/tracker/trackers/time.md) holds the procedure.
- The ticket moved to the state your project uses for integrated work, in the tracker its key belongs to, and the close recorded in persistent memory with its pull request and merge commit. Nothing is archived and no spec is merged afterward: a change to a contract a spec describes edits that spec in the work's own pull request.
- Merged branches and their clean working copies removed in each repository the work touched (the agent supplies eligible branches and active-work protections to `cleanup_branches.py`, then checks its plan before applying): the work's own branch and the earlier merged branches you authored, unless a working copy holds files that are not committed, a process is running in it, a pull request still uses the branch, or the branch belongs to work still in progress or whose close has waited less than a day; an older pending close is resumed rather than skipped. Your local copy of the base branch brought up to date with the remote, by fast-forward only, when that copy is on the base and nothing local is in the way: when the incoming changes touch a file you changed, left untracked or ignored, or the local base has commits of its own, it stays where it is and the report says why. Temporary files deleted, the processes stopped, and the containers it started (with their volumes) and the images it built removed. A dump or export it made is listed and removed only if you say so. Resources it created outside your machine, such as storage objects or registry images, are listed with the command that removes each, and removed only if you say so. Anything with uncommitted work or uncertain ownership is kept, and each kept branch is named in the report as kept with its reason. Full clones of the repository left in its working-copy folders, which Git does not list as working copies, are reported with their branch and unpublished commits and never removed.
- The work's support material handled without a question: verification material the ticket or the merged pull request already holds is deleted; the rest stays unversioned, moved out of a temporary working copy into your main checkout before that copy is removed; a conclusion worth documenting is named in the report with the project-layout command. The plan file always stays, in the work's folder under `workspace/`, until project-layout prunes it.
- What the work leaves open on purpose, such as a measurement over later CI runs or a later phase, written as a comment on the ticket and as one `Pending:` record in persistent memory that [Status](/flows/status/) lists. A promotion along your environments is not recorded this way, since the ticket's state already shows it, and one that has not been made keeps no branch, working copy or file; only a promotion that the plan itself lists as a task, because the ticket or your request asked for that environment, is recorded. Each later close updates the records that name its ticket and deletes one with nothing left open.
- The plan file never committed. A paused plan also stays local, so the report lists it with the rest of what is open.
- The rows of your project's account index, without any secret value, copied to persistent memory, so a later session or worktree knows which test accounts exist and where their secrets are, as the [test accounts rule](https://github.com/JhonHawk/tricell-hive/blob/master/content/skills/hive-core/local/test-accounts.md) sets.
- One final report of the whole work item, the only one. Close writes it from what build, delivery and deployment hand over: what was integrated, how each acceptance criterion was shown, what was skipped or could not run, local repairs, the work's support material with what was done to it, the findings, the candidates, the principles line, which Close takes from the checkpoint before removing it, and what remains.
- The candidates: each repetition the work did not turn into a mechanism, such as an inline script it ran two or more times for the same purpose or a correction you gave twice, listed without a question, with the mechanism that would replace it. Each one is recorded in the project's persistent memory with the sessions that saw it, so a repetition that comes back in a later session is offered in the footer.
- A page version of the report through [create-report](https://github.com/JhonHawk/tricell-hive/blob/master/content/skills/create-report/SKILL.md), offered as an option when you want to keep or share it.

When the work had captures in the viewer, Close finishes it first; see [Viewer](/viewer/). That only marks the work's record: Close never stops the viewer or closes the window you have open, nor quits a program other sessions may use, such as Docker Desktop or a local database server. When the viewer is not running, Close goes on without it, keeps what the viewer close needs, and the report says which viewer session stayed open.

## How it ends

As the close's situation sets. A close at the completion point, or of an operational task, ends with the footer, which offers closing only while something is left to run; your reply, including choosing to close, decides what runs next. The follow-up close of a pull request that waited, abandoned work, a deploy's cleanup close, a session-hygiene request or the session's end for work not yet closed, any close under unattended delegation, and work on hold or paused outside it end with the report alone. A session-hygiene request or the session's end after the work's close reports only what it ran, without a second final report. The report is the row of the [close report](https://github.com/JhonHawk/tricell-hive/blob/master/content/skills/hive-core/close/close-report.md#variants) that fits the delivery mode in force or the flow that called the close, and the footer and its replies are in [report endings](https://github.com/JhonHawk/tricell-hive/blob/master/content/skills/hive-core/messages/report-endings.md). The exact steps are in the [flow-close skill](https://github.com/JhonHawk/tricell-hive/blob/master/content/skills/flow-close/SKILL.md), which links the [close modes](https://github.com/JhonHawk/tricell-hive/blob/master/content/skills/hive-core/close/close-modes.md), the severity scale for findings in the [close report](https://github.com/JhonHawk/tricell-hive/blob/master/content/skills/hive-core/close/close-report.md), and the branch cleanup rules in its [branch cleanup reference](https://github.com/JhonHawk/tricell-hive/blob/master/content/skills/flow-close/references/branch-cleanup.md).

## What it does not do

- It does not delete a branch with unmerged commits, a branch just created with no commits of its own, or a working copy with uncommitted files; it keeps them and reports why. It never switches a checkout's branch.
- It does not push, merge or deploy on its own.
- It does not commit or archive the plan file; it stays local.

## Related flows

- [Verify](/flows/verify/) usually comes right before Close, because it integrates the change; [Build](/flows/build/) does when verify is skipped. Both end by running Close.
- [Deploy](/flows/deploy/) comes after integration. When it runs, only the cleanup of Close follows it.
- [Changes and loops](/flows/overview/) shows where the work goes when your answer adds to it.

## FAQ

### What if I only want to stop for now?
Say you are pausing. The pause also records in the plan's checkpoint what no file holds yet (see the [checkpoint skill](https://github.com/JhonHawk/tricell-hive/blob/master/content/skills/hive-core/plan/checkpoint.md)), and makes no `wip:` commit. The plan file stays local, and the report lists it with the rest of what is open.

### Does Close mean the work is shipped?
It means the work reached its completion point, for example merged into the base branch. Promotion to other environments is [Deploy](/flows/deploy/).
